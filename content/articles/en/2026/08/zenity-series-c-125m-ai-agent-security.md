## What happened

Zenity, which builds security and governance tools for autonomous AI
agents, raised a $125 million Series C round led by Norwest Venture
Partners, announced 3 August 2026 [1][2]. New investors SoftBank Vision
Fund 2, Hitachi Ventures, and LG Technology Ventures joined the round,
alongside returning investors Qumra Capital, Vertex Ventures, Third
Point Ventures, DTCP, and Intel Capital, taking Zenity's total funding
to about $185 million [1][2].

Founded in 2021 by Ben Kliger (CEO) and Michael Bargury (CTO), both
veterans of Israel's Unit 8200 who previously led cloud and operational
security work at Microsoft, Zenity now employs more than 230 people,
about 150 of them in Israel [1]. The company says its annual revenue
has tripled in each of the past two years and is on track to triple
again this year [1].

Zenity says it will use the new funding to expand its platform
capabilities, grow its Zenity Labs research division, and accelerate
international expansion in Europe and Asia-Pacific, building on
existing client presence in Japan, Korea, and Singapore [1][2].

## Why it matters

As companies deploy more autonomous AI agents that can take actions
rather than just generate text, a market has emerged for tools that
monitor what those agents actually do — not just what they were
instructed to do — and flag when they deviate from their intended
scope. SoftBank's, Hitachi's, and LG's strategic participation signals
established enterprise-technology players see AI-agent oversight as
infrastructure worth backing, not just a niche security product.
