## What happened

AI-inference chip startup Etched raised $700 million in a new funding
round, reaching a $21 billion valuation, the company announced 18 August
2026 [1][2][3]. The round was led by Jane Street, with Kleiner Perkins,
Sequoia Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, Tiger Global, Bain Capital Ventures,
Neo, Primary, Stripes, Positive Sum, and Blackstone also participating
[1][2][3].

The new valuation is roughly double the $10.3 billion Etched reached in a
$300 million Series C closed 23 July 2026 — a jump in just 26 days
[1][2][3]. It is the same larger, Jane Street-led round the Wall Street
Journal reported in mid-July was in talks at roughly $20 billion while
still unclosed; that round has now closed at $21 billion [1][2][3][4].

Jane Street, now Etched's first customer, has taken delivery of and put
into production the company's first hardware rack [1][2][3]. "Jane Street
putting this cluster into production is proof of what we've built,"
Etched co-founder and CEO Gavin Uberti said [1]. Etched says it has
secured more than $1 billion in customer contracts across AI companies
and cloud providers — a company-reported figure, not independently
audited [1].

Etched's valuation has now roughly quadrupled in eight months: $5 billion
in December 2025, $10.3 billion in July 2026, $21 billion in August 2026
[1][2][3].

## Why it matters

Etched is betting that chips purpose-built for AI inference — running
trained models, rather than training them — can beat general-purpose GPUs
like Nvidia's on cost and efficiency. A live production deployment at
Jane Street, rather than funding-round numbers alone, is the first
concrete sign of that bet being tested by a paying customer, even as the
company's broader commercial traction remains a company-reported figure
rather than an independently confirmed one.
