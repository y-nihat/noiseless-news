## What happened

Nvidia announced non-binding memoranda of understanding with six asset
managers — Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and
KKR — on 10 August 2026, to establish "AI Compute Infrastructure Financing
Platforms" aiming to mobilize over $500 billion in third-party capital for
data-center and GPU-cluster construction [1][2]. The agreements are MOUs
subject to definitive agreements, not committed or signed capital [1]. In a
CNBC interview alongside leaders of the six firms, CEO Jensen Huang said
Nvidia's own financial backstop would be capped at roughly 25% of any
individual project's financing, with the six firms making their own
independent lending decisions rather than relying on Nvidia's involvement to
replace their underwriting [3]. Nvidia's announcement describes the
platforms as financing for frontier AI labs, enterprises and AI clouds
generally, without naming OpenAI or any specific project — a distinct
initiative from Nvidia's earlier, still-unconfirmed talks to guarantee
around $250 billion in financing for OpenAI's Ohio data-center project,
first reported in late July 2026 [1][4].

## Why it matters

The platform is aimed at companies that want to build AI data centers but
lack the credit rating or balance sheet to finance them alone, extending
Nvidia's role from chip supplier toward enabling its customers' access to
capital. Because the deals are MOUs rather than committed funding, the
$500 billion figure is a mobilization target, not money already
transferred or contracted.
