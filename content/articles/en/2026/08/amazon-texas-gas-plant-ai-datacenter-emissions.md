## What happened

Amazon is financing a 7.65-gigawatt natural gas power plant with 35 turbines,
developed by Pacifico Energy under the name "GW Ranch," to power a planned AI
data center campus in Pecos County, Texas [1][2]. The plant is designed to
run off-grid rather than draw from the Texas grid at first, with Amazon
saying it is "designed to transition to grid-connected service as
interconnection timelines allow" [1][2].

Permit filings reviewed by market-intelligence firm Cleanview -- which
connected three Amazon-filed data center construction permits to the gas
plant using satellite imagery -- show the facility is authorized to emit
more than 30 million metric tons of carbon dioxide a year. Cleanview's
analysis, first reported by the New York Times and relayed by both outlets
cited here, found that would make it the single largest permitted emissions
source at any power plant currently operating in the US [1][2].

Amazon confirmed it is financing the plant but did not dispute the emissions
figures. A company spokesperson said Amazon "believes in paying the full
costs of powering our operations," adding "the world looks different now
than when we co-founded the climate pledge" and "our commitment hasn't
changed" [1].

## Why it matters

The project is the latest example of a tech company building its own power
plant to bring AI infrastructure online faster than grid interconnection
allows, at the cost of new, long-lived fossil-fuel generation. It sits
awkwardly against Amazon's own pledge to reach net-zero carbon emissions by
2040 [1][2].
