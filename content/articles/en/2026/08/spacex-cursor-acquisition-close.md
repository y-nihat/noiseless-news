## What happened

SpaceX's acquisition of Anysphere, the company behind the AI coding tool
Cursor, became effective on 14 August, according to a SpaceX regulatory
filing. The deal is structured as an all-stock merger: SpaceX's subsidiary
X67 Inc. merged into Anysphere, which is now a wholly owned SpaceX
subsidiary. SpaceX issued roughly 391 million Class A shares to cover
Anysphere's outstanding stock and vested equity awards, priced off the
seven-day average of SpaceX's own stock before closing -- implying a $60.0
billion equity value for Anysphere, plus further shares reserved for
unvested options and RSUs.

The transaction traces back to April 2026, when SpaceX secured an option to
acquire Anysphere, and 16 June, when it exercised that option and announced
the deal publicly, four days after SpaceX's own initial public offering.
Cursor confirmed the close in a blog post, saying it now has access to
SpaceX's compute infrastructure, including its Colossus GPU cluster --
which it describes, in its own words, as the largest fleet of GPUs in the
world. Cursor says it will keep operating with the same product focus.

## Why it matters

The deal folds one of the most widely used AI coding tools -- Cursor says
it is used by tens of thousands of businesses -- into SpaceX's compute
buildout, giving Anysphere a captive source of GPU capacity at a moment
when access to training and inference hardware is a binding constraint for
AI labs. It also extends SpaceX's push beyond rockets and satellites into
AI products, following its earlier merger with xAI, whose AI work now
operates under the SpaceX umbrella.
