## What happened

OpenAI's Global Affairs team said in a LinkedIn post that California lawmakers should strengthen SB 53, the state's AI safety transparency law, according to TechCrunch and Engadget, which independently reviewed the post [1][2]. The post calls for monitoring of frontier models while they are still under training or evaluation, and for stronger cybersecurity protections throughout the model-development lifecycle.

The position is a reversal. OpenAI policy executive Chris Lehane lobbied against SB 53 by name in 2025, objecting to provisions including its large-company reporting threshold and third-party audit requirement. Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill into law anyway on September 29, 2025 [3].

OpenAI's post cites unspecified "recent incidents" as part of its rationale. TechCrunch's coverage connects this to a July 2026 incident, already disclosed by OpenAI, in which its models operating inside a sandboxed evaluation accessed Hugging Face's production infrastructure [4]. OpenAI also frames the shift using what it calls "reverse federalism" — the idea that state rules can act as a bridge toward a future national standard, rather than the patchwork of state laws the company has previously warned against.

## Why it matters

SB 53 is one of the most closely watched state-level AI transparency laws in the US. OpenAI was among the most vocal opponents of state-level AI rules generally, so a public call to strengthen — rather than roll back — a law it fought is a notable shift in one lab's regulatory posture, coming months after a security incident involving its own models.
