## What happened

Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro signed Executive Order 2026-05,
"Protecting Pennsylvania Consumers from Data Center Impacts," on 18 August
2026 [1][2][3]. The order bars the state Department of Environmental
Protection from issuing a data center permit unless the project has already
received all required local government approvals [1][3]. It also removes AI
data center projects from Pennsylvania's Permit Fast Track Program, bars
state agencies from signing non-disclosure agreements with developers, and
conditions new permits on the state's "GRID" standards — covering how
energy costs are passed on to ratepayers, water use, community benefit
agreements and environmental review [1][4]. The conditions apply to permit
applications submitted after 18 August; projects that already hold approvals
keep them, but must meet new reporting and transparency rules [5].

In his signing remarks, Shapiro called the order "the nation's strictest
guardrails on AI data centers" [2]. Critics disputed that framing from the
opposite direction: Democratic state Senator Katie Muth said the order falls
short of what is needed and has pushed instead for a multi-year moratorium
on new construction, while industry representatives objected that it turns
previously voluntary commitments into binding requirements for projects
already under way [3][4].

Pennsylvania is the third US state this year to take gubernatorial executive
action restricting data center development. New York Governor Kathy Hochul
signed a temporary permit moratorium in July for data centers of 50
megawatts or more [7]. Texas Governor Greg Abbott ordered grid regulators to
halt new data center interconnection approvals pending a state audit in
early August [8][9]. The three states used different mechanisms — a
temporary pause in New York, a grid-connection freeze in Texas, and a
standing local-approval requirement with no fixed end date in Pennsylvania.

## Why it matters

The order is Pennsylvania's response to a data center construction wave
driven substantially by AI compute demand, and it takes effect immediately
under the governor's executive authority rather than waiting for
legislation [1][2]. That also means it rests on a foundation a future
governor could undo more easily than a statute — Shapiro has separately
asked the legislature to codify the standards into law, which has not
happened [2][3].
