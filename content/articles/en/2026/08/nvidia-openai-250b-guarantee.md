## What happened

Nvidia will guarantee up to $105 billion in lease-residual-value obligations
for OpenAI's new data-center campus in Pike County, Ohio, according to
Nvidia's own SEC filing and press release, independently confirmed the same
day by Reuters, Bloomberg, CNBC, TechCrunch and Nikkei Asia
[1][2][3][4][5][6][7]. Nvidia will separately invest $1.5 billion directly
in SB Energy, the SoftBank-owned company developing the site — additive to,
not part of, the guarantee [1][2][5].

The campus, called the PORTS-Pike Technology Campus, is planned for 4.25
gigawatts of initial capacity with an option to extend by a further 3.75
gigawatts, for up to 8 gigawatts total, coming online in phases starting in
2028 [1][2][3]. SB Energy will build, own and operate the site; OpenAI has
signed a 20-year lease. Nvidia's guarantee is a residual-value backstop — it
pays out only if OpenAI defaults and the site cannot be re-leased or sold at
full value, not an upfront cash payment [1][2][3].

Nvidia and OpenAI project the build will support about 35,000 construction
jobs through 2032 and about 2,500 permanent operating jobs; SB Energy and
SoftBank plan more than $4.2 billion in regional grid upgrades with utility
AEP Ohio. A separate $80 million community-benefits fund is split evenly
between SB Energy and OpenAI — Nvidia is not a funder of it [1][2][3].

## Why it matters

Today's figures close out three weeks of unverified reporting. Reports
attributed to The Wall Street Journal had put the guarantee at around $250
billion in late July, then under $120 billion as of 14 August; neither
figure carried independent corroboration [8]. The confirmed $105 billion
guarantee is a separate financing mechanism from Nvidia's 10 August
memoranda of understanding with six Wall Street asset managers to mobilize
over $500 billion in AI data-center financing generally — no source
describes the Ohio campus as funded through that platform [1]. Together,
the two announcements show Nvidia extending its balance sheet across
separately structured deals to help fund the data-center buildout its own
chip sales depend on.
