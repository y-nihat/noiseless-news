## What happened

wikiHow, Inc. filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, Inc. and eight affiliated
entities in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York on
21 August 2026, case no. 1:2026cv07171, alleging copyright infringement and
violations of the Digital Millennium Copyright Act [1][2][3].

The complaint alleges that OpenAI trained ChatGPT on wikiHow's how-to
articles obtained through large-scale crawling and scraping, and that
OpenAI's systems separately retrieve and reproduce wikiHow content through
retrieval-augmented generation at runtime [1][2]. wikiHow also alleges OpenAI
stripped copyright management information -- bylines, titles, dates and
terms-of-use notices -- from the copied articles, a separate DMCA violation
[1][2].

According to the complaint, wikiHow's site logged more than 185,000 visits
from OpenAI's published IP ranges between March and May 2025, and OpenAI's
crawlers reached the site 148,529 times between May and July 2026 alone. The
suit covers 1,211 of wikiHow's registered copyrights, spanning 11,211
articles [1][2]. wikiHow says it approached OpenAI about a license in spring
2024 and that OpenAI never seriously pursued one [1][2]. The complaint seeks
an injunction and damages; OpenAI had made no public statement on the suit as
of publication [1].

## Why it matters

wikiHow joins a growing list of publishers -- including the New York Times,
Encyclopaedia Britannica and Merriam-Webster -- suing OpenAI over training
data, but its complaint adds a runtime dimension: it alleges ChatGPT keeps
drawing on wikiHow's content live, through retrieval-augmented generation,
not only through data absorbed during training. The allegations are
wikiHow's own and untested in court; whether OpenAI's use qualifies as fair
use is exactly the question this case will decide.
