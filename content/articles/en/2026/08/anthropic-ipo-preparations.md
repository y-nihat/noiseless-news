## What happened

Anthropic confidentially filed paperwork for an IPO in June 2026 and has since held preparatory meetings with banks and investors, with a public filing possible as soon as late August [1][2]. Its annualized revenue run-rate reached about $65 billion in July, up from about $47 billion in May [3][4].

Bloomberg reports the offering could be sized to match or exceed SpaceX's record IPO: about $75 billion at the base offering, rising to roughly $86 billion if an overallotment option is exercised, at a valuation of about $1.77 trillion [5]. That figure comes from Bloomberg's reporting alone and has not been independently reproduced elsewhere.

Separately, CNBC reports, citing people familiar with the matter, that the filing is expected to list public backlash against AI and opposition to new data centers as a risk factor -- pointing to investor questions fielded by CFO Krishna Rao and a Gallup finding that roughly seven in ten Americans oppose a data center being built near them [6]. That, too, is a single outlet's sourced characterization of what the filing will show, not a direct read of the filing text itself, and no other outlet has independently corroborated it.

Anthropic has not publicly confirmed if or when it will go public and declined to comment on both the revenue figures and the risk-factor reporting [2][6].

## Why it matters

An IPO at this scale would rank among the largest in history and would be the clearest signal yet of how the AI boom is being priced by Wall Street, not just by venture investors. The specific size and valuation, and the risk factors Anthropic itself will disclose, remain single-outlet reporting until a public filing or additional independent confirmation lands -- worth tracking, not yet worth treating as settled.
