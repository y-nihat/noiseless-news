## What happened

Cohere and the University of Waterloo said on 6 August 2026 that they have
partnered to build a non-credit "AI Transformation and Change Management"
certificate, led by Waterloo's Future of Work Institute and launching in
fall 2026 [1][2]. The curriculum covers AI literacy, human-centered design,
ethics, business strategy, and change management, combining classroom
learning with workplace experience [1][2]. The first cohort is scheduled to
start in January 2027, with 20 co-op positions — open to students from any
faculty — placing participants with private, public-sector, and non-profit
organizations to work on AI use-case discovery and implementation [2]. The
program also draws on Waterloo's Centre for Work-Integrated Learning and its
GreenHouse Social Impact Incubator [2].

Cohere Chief AI Officer Joelle Pineau said Waterloo "has played a defining
role" in building Canada's AI talent pipeline [2]. Future of Work Institute
executive director Edith Law said "AI adoption is not the point, but solving
problems is" [2]. Neither party disclosed a dollar figure for the
partnership [1][2].

## Why it matters

The deal gives Cohere a second marquee Canadian university partnership this
summer, after a similar July arrangement with the University of Toronto,
as the company positions itself around Canadian AI talent and "sovereign
AI." It is a joint announcement by the two parties rather than independently
reported news, and the certificate itself does not launch its first cohort
until January 2027 [1][2].
