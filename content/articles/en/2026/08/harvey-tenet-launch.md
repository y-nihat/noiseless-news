## What happened

Harvey, a legal AI company whose products are used by large law firms,
announced on 18 August 2026 that it has built "Harvey Tenet," which it
describes as the company's first AI model developed and post-trained
specifically for legal reasoning, rather than one adapted from a
general-purpose foundation model built by another company [1]. The
announcement is part of a wider platform update Harvey calls "Harvey II,"
which also adds persistent memory and personalization features across Word,
Outlook and Harvey's AI agents [1]. Until now, Harvey said, its products
have relied on general-purpose models from outside vendors; the company had
publicly signaled this in-house research effort for months before today's
release [1]. The launch was independently reported the same day by
legal-trade outlet Global Legal Post [2].

Harvey's own announcement says Tenet performs at a "frontier level" on the
company's legal benchmarks, "on par with the strongest general models," at
what it describes as open-source-level cost [1]. That comparison comes
entirely from Harvey's own evaluation suite; no independent reproduction of
the result has been published.

## Why it matters

Harvey has built its business on top of third-party foundation models from
vendors including OpenAI and Anthropic; shipping a proprietary,
task-specific model moves part of its technology stack in-house. Because
Tenet's benchmark results come from Harvey's own evaluation set rather than
an independent test, they describe what Harvey says about its own product,
not a result confirmed by a third party.
