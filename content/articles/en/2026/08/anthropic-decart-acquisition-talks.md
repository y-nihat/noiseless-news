## What happened

Anthropic is in talks to acquire Decart, a Tel Aviv-based AI startup, in
what would be Anthropic's largest acquisition to date [1][2]. Bloomberg and
Calcalist each reported the talks independently around 13 August 2026,
both citing people familiar with the discussions and both putting the
price at roughly $6 billion [1][2]. Both outlets stressed that the talks
were at an early stage and could still collapse; Anthropic declined to
comment and Decart did not respond to requests for comment [1][2].

As of 16 August, no agreement has been signed [1][2][3]. Calcalist reported
that day that the price under discussion has since risen to about $7
billion, that Anthropic's offer beat a higher competing bid from Nvidia,
and that Decart's founders and its lead investor, Sequoia Capital, favor
Anthropic over the alternatives -- attributing these details to its own
sources [3]. No other outlet has yet corroborated the $7 billion figure,
the Nvidia comparison, or the founders'/Sequoia's stated preference, so
those specifics are reported here as Calcalist's account, not as
independently confirmed fact.

Decart builds an optimization engine that improves the efficiency of AI
chips, cutting the cost of training and running models, along with two
"world models": Lucy, which edits video in real time, and Oasis, which
generates simulated environments used to train and test robotics and
autonomous-driving systems [1][2]. Founded in 2023, the company raised a
$300 million Series B in May 2026 at a valuation of about $4 billion, up
from $3.1 billion in August 2025; Nvidia itself was among the investors in
that round [1][2].

## Why it matters

A deal of this size would be Anthropic's largest acquisition as the
company builds out infrastructure ahead of a planned Nasdaq listing, and
it would fold a chip-efficiency specialist directly into Anthropic's own
compute stack at a time of intense demand for inference capacity. That
Nvidia was reportedly also bidding for Decart -- while remaining one of
Decart's existing investors -- underscores how contested access to
efficiency gains on top of scarce AI chips has become. The deal is not yet
signed, so the outcome, and the accuracy of Calcalist's higher $7 billion
figure, remain open questions.
