## What happened

The European Commission said on 30 July 2026 that it has opened a formal
call for tenders to build up to seven "AI Gigafactories" across the EU —
large compute facilities combining AI processors, software, cloud
infrastructure and energy-efficient data centres, meant to give startups,
scale-ups, industry, academia and public bodies access to infrastructure for
training, running and fine-tuning frontier AI models [1][2]. The program
complements an existing network of 19 smaller "AI Factories" already
running [1]. The call closes 12 November 2026, with site selections expected
in early 2027 [1].

The Commission is backing the program with up to €10 billion in EU and
national public funding, which it projects will unlock at least €20 billion
in private investment — more than €30 billion in total [1][2]. That private-
investment figure is the Commission's own projection tied to the tender's
outcome, not money already committed [1].

## Why it matters

This is the EU's largest single push yet to build AI compute capacity on
European soil, aimed at reducing reliance on US and Chinese infrastructure
for training frontier models. As with any government funding call, the
private-investment total is a projection, not a confirmed commitment; the
actual scale will depend on which bids the Commission selects and whether
they close in early 2027 as planned.
