## What happened

OpenAI said on 7 August 2026 that internal evaluations of Astra, an
unreleased model, showed advances in agentic coding and cybersecurity
significant enough that the company "cannot rule out" Astra has reached
the "Critical" tier for cyber capabilities in its Preparedness
Framework [1]. It is the first time any OpenAI model has been flagged
at that level specifically for cybersecurity [1][2][3]. The company has
not confirmed Astra has actually crossed the threshold — testing is
ongoing.

Under the framework, a model hits the Critical cyber threshold if it
can independently find and build working zero-day exploits across many
hardened, real-world systems, or can plan and carry out an entire novel
cyberattack against a hardened target from nothing more than a
high-level goal [1].

In response, OpenAI says it has paused internal activities involving
Astra that lack adequate safeguards, moved testing into isolated
environments with restricted network and tool access, added
universal monitoring of the model's actions, and is bringing in
government agencies and outside AI-safety organizations to evaluate it
further before any release decision [1][2][3]. Astra, first named
publicly by OpenAI on 1 August 2026 in connection with unrelated
mathematics research, is unrelated to Google's separate "Project
Astra" despite the shared name.

## Why it matters

This is the first model to trigger OpenAI's own top cybersecurity
tier since the company published its Preparedness Framework, a marker
the industry has been watching for as models get better at finding and
exploiting software vulnerabilities. Independent confirmation that
Astra has actually crossed the line is not yet available — the
classification and the response are OpenAI's own, self-reported ahead
of any external audit result.
