## What happened

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei posted a multi-part reply on X on 15 August
2026 to Gavin Baker, chief investment officer of Atreides Management, who
had publicly argued -- including in remarks on the All-In podcast -- that
Amodei's repeated warnings about AI risk were fueling a broader backlash
against AI companies and data-center construction [1][2][3].

Amodei wrote that the backlash is "fundamentally a crisis of trust,"
adding that "by far the most accurate criticism of AI companies including
Anthropic is that we haven't yet delivered on our big promises to benefit
the world," and that "the thing that will work is actually curing cancer"
[1][2][3]. He argued open-weight model releases, while helpful, "are
nowhere near a sufficient solution" to that trust problem, because they
mainly shift the concentration of power toward whoever controls the most
computing power and chips rather than removing the concentration itself
[1][2].

Amodei also pushed back on what he called a "Silicon Valley shorthand"
that treats AI regulation as inherently equivalent to regulatory capture
and the concentration of power, calling that view "an overly simplified
picture of the world." He said Anthropic tries to design its regulatory
proposals to slow down or constrain frontier AI companies specifically
while leaving room for smaller competitors, pointing to the company's
support for California's SB 53 transparency law, pre-deployment testing
of both frontier and near-frontier open-weight models, and Demis
Hassabis's proposal for a FINRA-like AI oversight body as examples [1][2].

## Why it matters

The exchange is notable less for new policy than for the dispute it
plays out in public: a frontier-lab CEO responding directly, by name, to
a prominent investor's charge that his own safety messaging is doing
harm. The positions Amodei restates here -- support for mandatory
third-party testing, transparency rules and a regulatory design that
weighs more heavily on the largest labs -- are consistent with ones
Anthropic has taken in public policy statements before; the news value is
the specific, on-the-record admission that criticism of AI companies for
not yet delivering promised benefits is "by far the most accurate," made
in direct response to a named, credentialed critic rather than in the
abstract.
