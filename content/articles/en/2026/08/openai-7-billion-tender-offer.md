## What happened

OpenAI completed a $7 billion tender offer on 10 August 2026, buying back
shares from current and former employees [1][2]. Unlike the company's two
previous employee liquidity rounds, OpenAI itself was the buyer this time,
rather than bringing in outside investors to purchase the shares [1][2]. The
deal valued OpenAI at $852 billion -- the same figure set by its record $122
billion primary funding round, which closed in March 2026 -- so the tender
does not represent a markup on the company's most recent valuation [1][2].

The report comes from Bloomberg, which cited a person familiar with the
matter, and CNBC, which separately reported the same figures under its own
byline the same day [1][2]. Neither outlet names an on-record source inside
OpenAI, and the company has not issued its own statement confirming the
tender [1][2].

OpenAI has run two earlier tenders: a $1.5 billion round in November 2024
led by SoftBank, and a $6.6 billion round in October 2025 in which more than
600 employees participated, with about 75 of them each selling roughly $30
million in shares [1][2]. Both of those rounds involved outside investors
buying the shares; this one did not [1][2].

## Why it matters

A company buying back its own employees' shares, rather than routing a
tender through outside investors, is a different signal than a markup round:
it provides liquidity without pulling in new capital or new investors, and it
leaves the valuation exactly where the March primary round set it. Combined
with OpenAI's confidential S-1 filing on record with the SEC, the buyback
structure reads as balance-sheet housekeeping ahead of a potential public
listing rather than a fresh vote of confidence from the market.
