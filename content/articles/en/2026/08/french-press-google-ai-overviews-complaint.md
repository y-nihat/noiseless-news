## What happened

The Alliance de la presse d'information générale (APIG), which represents
about 300 French daily newspapers, filed a formal complaint with France's
Autorité de la concurrence on 11 August 2026, targeting Google's AI
Overviews and AI Mode features [1][2]. APIG alleges that Google rolled out
the AI-generated summaries in French search results without prior
authorization or negotiated payment, which it says breaches commitments
Google made in 2022 under France's neighboring-rights framework — a legal
mechanism, stemming from the EU Copyright Directive, meant to force
platforms to negotiate compensation with publishers for use of their
content [1]. APIG president Marc Feuillée said the association was
presented with a "fait accompli," with the only alternative being a
technical withdrawal that would cost publishers visibility in search
results [1].

APIG also says AI-generated summaries have already cut referral traffic to
member sites by between 33% and 38%, citing an estimate from Arcom,
France's audiovisual regulator; that figure has not been independently
verified against Arcom's own published data [2]. None of these
allegations have been adjudicated: the Autorité de la concurrence has not
yet ruled on the complaint, and no timeline for a decision has been
announced.

Google responded that it has changed how it accounts for protected
content displayed within AI Overviews and AI Mode, and said publishers can
choose not to have their content appear in those features; the company
also said it had notified more than 450 French publishers about its
approach beforehand [3]. The complaint follows an 8 July 2026 ruling in
which the same authority ordered Meta to resume stalled negotiations with
publishers over AI-related content use, a precedent APIG has cited as
grounds for similar action against Google [1].

## Why it matters

This is the first formal competition-authority complaint targeting
Google's AI Overviews specifically, rather than search practices in
general, and tests whether France's 2022 neighboring-rights settlement —
which already produced a €250 million fine against Google in 2024 [2] —
extends to AI-generated summaries. A ruling either way would set a
reference point for how EU competition law treats AI search features that
draw on publisher content, beyond France.
