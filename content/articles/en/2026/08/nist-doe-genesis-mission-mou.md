## What happened

NIST said on 4 August 2026 that it signed a memorandum of understanding
with the Department of Energy's Office of Science to collaborate on
AI-accelerated innovation as part of the Genesis Mission, the White
House-led initiative aiming to double the productivity and impact of
American science and engineering within a decade [1][2]. The Genesis
Mission, launched by executive order in November 2025, is a whole-of-
government push spanning multiple agencies and national labs; Meta
separately detailed its own role deploying vision models on DOE
supercomputers for a related Berkeley Lab-led project, SYNAPS-I, in July.

Under the MOU, NIST will run two initiatives through its Centers for AI
in Manufacturing and Critical Infrastructure — a public-private
partnership with MITRE Corporation. The first, an AI Economic Security
Center for U.S. Manufacturing Productivity, targets autonomous agents
and AI-based robotics for manufacturing, including a drone-production
project aiming for a tenfold scale-up within two years. The second, an
AI Economic Security Center to Secure U.S. Critical Infrastructure from
Cyberthreats, develops AI agents for rapid detection and response to
cyberthreats across power grids, telecommunications networks, water
treatment facilities, financial platforms and healthcare systems [1].
Both are structured as two-year sprints intended to produce results
that can be replicated across other manufacturing and cybersecurity
domains. NIST Director Arvind Raman said: "Our contribution to the
Genesis Mission exemplifies NIST's ongoing commitment to advancing U.S.
leadership in critical technologies" [1].

## Why it matters

The MOU extends Genesis Mission's reach from basic science at DOE
national labs into manufacturing productivity and critical-infrastructure
cybersecurity, two economically sensitive areas where the administration
is betting AI agents can deliver fast, measurable gains within a two-year
window.
