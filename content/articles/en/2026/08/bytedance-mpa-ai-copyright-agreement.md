## What happened

The Motion Picture Association and ByteDance signed a Memorandum of
Understanding on 17 August 2026 establishing guardrails intended to
protect film and television intellectual property from ByteDance's
generative AI video and image models, deployed through TikTok, CapCut,
Dreamina and the TikTok USDS joint venture [1][2]. MPA chairman and CEO
Charles Rivkin said the agreement "illustrates our belief that copyright
is a cornerstone of the film and television industry — and reinforces our
commitment to protect creative content" [1]. ByteDance general counsel
John Rogovin said the company "respects the intellectual property rights
that underpin creative industries around the world" [1]. The MPA's press
release does not disclose a specific enforcement mechanism, license
terms, or technical thresholds [1].

The agreement follows a cease-and-desist letter the MPA sent ByteDance in
February 2026, after its Seedance 2.0 model was used to generate
unauthorized video clips depicting copyrighted characters and celebrity
likenesses, including of Brad Pitt and Tom Cruise [3].

## Why it matters

The agreement replaces confrontation with a negotiated framework: in
February, the MPA's move was a cease-and-desist letter; in August, both
sides describe a collaborative agreement [1][3]. But the MOU is not a
binding contract, and because neither side has published enforcement
terms or license conditions, what it will concretely change about what
ByteDance's models can generate is not yet known [1].
