## What happened

Starcloud, a startup building data centers designed to operate in orbit,
announced on 21 August 2026 that it raised $250 million in a new funding
round at a $2.3 billion valuation [1][2]. The round was led by Manhattan
West, with Nvidia joining as a new investor [1][2]; TechCrunch reported
Nvidia's specific investment at approximately $25 million [2]. The raise
extends a $170 million Series A that TechCrunch reported Starcloud closed
in March 2026 [2].

Starcloud said the funding will support its plan to deploy Nvidia's
upcoming Vera Rubin-generation chips in orbital data centers, targeting a
launch around 2028 [1][2]. The company's CEO told TechCrunch the timeline
is shaped by limited launch capacity, since SpaceX's Falcon 9 program is
scheduled to end in 2028 [2].

## Why it matters

It's one of the largest funding rounds yet for orbital AI compute, a
category that remains unproven at commercial scale. Nvidia's participation
as an investor — on top of its existing role as a chip supplier — gives it
a direct financial stake in whether space-based data centers become
viable, at a moment when launch-capacity constraints are shaping the
timeline [2].
