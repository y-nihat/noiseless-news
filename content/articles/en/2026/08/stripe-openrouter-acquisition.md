## What happened

Stripe said on 19 August 2026 that it has agreed to acquire OpenRouter, a
New York-based startup that operates a marketplace routing requests across
hundreds of third-party AI models [1]. OpenRouter confirmed the deal in a
matching announcement on its own blog, saying it is "joining forces with
Stripe," with the transaction "subject to customary closing conditions" and
closing expected "in the coming weeks" [2]. Neither company disclosed
financial terms [1][2].

Media reports on the price disagree. Bloomberg first reported the deal, on
16 August, at "over $7 billion" [3]. The New York Times later put the figure
at about $7.5 billion, split between OpenRouter's founders and investors --
a number CNBC's coverage attributes to the Times rather than reporting
independently [4]. Other outlets have cited over $8 billion. None of these
figures has been confirmed by Stripe or OpenRouter.

Stripe CEO Patrick Collison said in the announcement: "Tokens are the
central currency for companies building with AI, and it's clear that the
real-world economic potential will depend on making good use of scarce
compute resources" [1].

## Why it matters

A payments company buying an AI model-routing startup signals Stripe sees
token and compute flows as adjacent to its core business, not just a
customer vertical. OpenRouter's reported price -- several times the roughly
$1.3 billion valuation it raised at only three months earlier -- reflects
how fast valuations are moving in AI infrastructure, though the exact
multiple depends on which reported figure holds up. Because the deal has not
closed and its price remains unconfirmed, both are open questions this site
will revisit.
