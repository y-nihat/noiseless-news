## What happened

Samsung Electronics and Broadcom signed a memorandum of understanding on 24
July 2026, at an AI summit in San Francisco, to expand their chip
partnership into a deal the two companies value at more than $200 billion
running through 2030 [1][2]. Under the agreement, Broadcom's
next-generation high-speed communications chips will be manufactured on
Samsung's sub-2-nanometer foundry process, with the two companies also
collaborating on advanced 2.3D and 2.5D chip packaging and on
next-generation high-bandwidth memory (HBM) [1][2]. Samsung said in a
statement that "the expanded collaboration ... reflects Samsung's focus on
supporting customers with end-to-end semiconductor technologies across an
increasingly diverse range of AI and high-performance computing
applications" [1]. No Broadcom statement or executive quote has
accompanied the announcement. As an MOU, the agreement is a statement of
intent rather than a signed, binding supply contract [1][2].

## Why it matters

The deal would make Broadcom, one of the AI industry's leading custom
chip designers, a major long-term customer for Samsung's foundry business
at a moment when Samsung is trying to close the manufacturing gap with
TSMC. It lands the same week Samsung Electronics and SK Group struck
separate large AI-infrastructure agreements with Nvidia, underscoring how
much of the current AI buildout is running through South Korean chip and
memory makers. Because the terms are an MOU rather than a finalized
contract, the $200 billion figure and the 2030 timeline are the companies'
own stated targets, not yet locked in by a binding agreement.
