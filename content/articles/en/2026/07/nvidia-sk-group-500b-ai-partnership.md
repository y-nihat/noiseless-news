## What happened

SK Group and Nvidia signed letters of intent on 24 July 2026 for a
partnership the two companies value at more than $500 billion, announced at
an AI summit in San Francisco [1][2]. The agreement is not a signed final
contract — both companies describe it as letters of intent covering two
linked commitments: AI data-center construction and long-term AI memory
supply [1][2].

Under the first piece, SK Telecom plans to build a 2-gigawatt AI factory in
South Korea built on Nvidia's DSX full-stack architecture, running Nvidia's
next-generation Vera Rubin compute platform on SK hynix's HBM4 memory, with
the first phase targeted to come online in 2027 [1][2]. Under the second,
SK hynix and Nvidia agreed separately to a long-term partnership to secure
a stable HBM supply pipeline for Nvidia and to jointly develop and optimize
future generations of AI memory, including for large language model
training, agentic AI and physical AI workloads [1][2]. Nvidia CEO Jensen
Huang said the companies "are building a new generation of AI factories
that will power Korea's next wave of growth"; SK Group Chairman Chey
Tae-won said SK would "collaborate with NVIDIA to build a world-class AI
factory" [1].

## Why it matters

The deal formalizes South Korea's push to become a major AI infrastructure
hub and locks in SK hynix — which supplies roughly 60% of the global HBM
market — as a preferred long-term memory partner for Nvidia at a moment
when HBM capacity is a binding constraint on AI data-center buildouts
industry-wide. Because the agreement rests on letters of intent rather than
finalized contracts, the $500 billion figure and 2027 timeline are the
companies' own stated commitments, not yet locked in by binding agreements.
