## What happened

The Bombay High Court on 27 July 2026 granted India's Union Minister for Road Transport and Highways, Nitin Gadkari, permission to file a civil suit against Meta, X Corp and Google (including YouTube), along with two Indian government departments, over AI-generated deepfake content [1][2][3]. Justice Abhay Ahuja's single-judge bench granted leave under Clause XII of the Letters Patent — a procedural step required because part of the alleged conduct originates outside the court's territorial jurisdiction [1][2].

Gadkari's plea alleges that unknown persons created and circulated deepfake videos and posts, identifying at least 24 of them, that falsely portray him as personally responsible for India's E20 ethanol-blended-petrol policy and claim that he and his family financially benefited from it [1][2][3]. Gadkari states the E20 programme is run by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, not his own ministry, and that he holds no administrative or financial role in it [1][2][3]. He is seeking roughly Rs 11 crore in damages, removal of the content, and an injunction against further unauthorised AI-generated use of his voice, image and likeness [1][2]. His plea states the suit does not aim to curtail fair public criticism or debate of the policy [1][2].

The 27 July order only permits the suit to be filed; it is not a ruling on whether the alleged content is defamatory or on any of Gadkari's claims. No interim takedown order was granted at this stage — that request, along with the substantive suit, will be heard later before Justice Arif Doctor [1][2]. Meta, X and Google have not yet issued a public response.

## Why it matters

A sitting Indian cabinet minister is pursuing Meta, X and Google directly over AI-generated deepfakes rather than relying solely on takedown requests, testing how Indian courts handle deepfake-defamation claims against global platforms. Because this is only a procedural order granting leave to sue, the underlying allegations remain unproven and contested; whether any deepfake content is actually defamatory, and whether the platforms bear liability, will depend on the later hearing.
