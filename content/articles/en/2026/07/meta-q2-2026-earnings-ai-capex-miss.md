## What happened

Meta's second-quarter 2026 earnings release, published 29 July 2026, reported
$60.8 billion in revenue, up 28% year over year, but net income fell 14% to
$15.8 billion [1]. Diluted earnings per share of $6.18 missed the average
analyst estimate of $7.17 [2][3]. Shares fell about 9.6% in after-hours
trading following the release [3].

The shortfall traces to AI infrastructure spending. Capital expenditure hit
$31.08 billion for the quarter, more than double the $17.0 billion Meta spent
in Q2 2025 [1][2]. The company narrowed its full-year 2026 capex guidance to
$130-145 billion, up from the prior $125-145 billion range it had given
previously [1][2][3]. CFO Susan Li said the guidance reflects a focus on
"maximizing 2026 and 2027 capacity" while preserving flexibility for later
years, and CEO Mark Zuckerberg said the company continues "to invest
aggressively in infrastructure to meet the demand" [3]. Separately, Meta's
Reality Labs division — the unit building the company's AR/VR and
metaverse products — posted a $4.62 billion operating loss on $431 million
in revenue, up from $370 million a year earlier [1][2].

## Why it matters

Meta is one of the largest single spenders in the AI infrastructure buildout,
and this quarter's results show that spending now weighing directly on
reported profit even as revenue keeps growing at double-digit rates. The
after-hours stock drop suggests investors are starting to price in the gap
between AI capital outlays and near-term returns — the same tension playing
out across the AI-chip and cloud-compute sector this week. Reality Labs'
continued losses are a reminder that Meta is running two AI-linked spending
programs at once, one on generative-AI infrastructure and one on its
longer-running metaverse bet.
