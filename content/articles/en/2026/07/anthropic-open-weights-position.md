## What happened

Anthropic published a statement on 27 July 2026 saying it has "never advocated for a ban on open-weights models" and does not support banning them as a category, calling safe open-weight models "a public good" [1]. CEO Dario Amodei wrote that Anthropic's primary national-security concern is authoritarian governments — he names China — building more powerful AI models than the US, not the existence of open-weight models themselves [1][2]. He named a secondary concern: open-weight models "do potentially present a higher risk than closed models" for misuse in cyberattacks or biological attacks, since safeguards are harder to apply once weights are released [1].

Rather than a ban, Anthropic's statement calls for three narrower measures: restricting powerful chip sales to China and cracking down on smuggling, cracking down on "industrial-scale distillation operations," and mandatory safety testing for "all sufficiently capable models, open and closed" [1].

The statement landed three days after Nvidia, Meta, Microsoft, Hugging Face, Mistral and other companies published a joint letter opposing "premature restrictions" on open-weight models, a letter Anthropic did not sign [2][3]. TechCrunch reported that Amodei's post, published Monday afternoon, was a direct response to industry murmuring about that absence [2].

## Why it matters

Anthropic has been the most vocal frontier lab about AI risk from authoritarian states, and its absence from the industry's pro-open-weight letter had been read as tacit support for restricting open models generally. This statement narrows that ambiguity: Anthropic is drawing a line between opposing a blanket ban and still pushing for chip-export controls, anti-distillation enforcement, and mandatory safety testing that would apply to open and closed models alike.
